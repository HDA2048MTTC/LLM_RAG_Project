Overview of Fiber Subtypes
This document describes key fiber subtypes, including cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, pectins, gums, beta-glucans, and resistant starch. People often ask which fiber types help with cholesterol, digestion, or gut health.

Insoluble Fiber Subtypes
Cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin are insoluble fiber subtypes. They add bulk to stool and help prevent constipation. These fibers are commonly found in whole grains, nuts, seeds, and vegetable skins.

Soluble Fiber Subtypes
Pectins and gums dissolve in water and help slow digestion. Beta-glucans, found in oats and barley, are especially effective at lowering cholesterol and are sometimes called “heart-healthy fibers.”

Fermentable and Functional Fibers
Some fibers—such as resistant starch, inulin, and fructooligosaccharides (FOS)—act as fermentable prebiotic fibers, feeding beneficial gut bacteria and supporting colon cell health. Functional fibers like psyllium are often added to foods or supplements and support digestion.
