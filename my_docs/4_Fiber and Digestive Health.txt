How Dietary Fiber Supports Digestion
This document explains how fiber improves digestion. Insoluble fiber adds bulk to stool, absorbs water, and helps the intestines move food efficiently. Many people increase insoluble fiber to relieve constipation.

Soluble Fiber for Gut Comfort
Soluble fiber slows digestion, supports nutrient absorption, and becomes food for gut bacteria. This is why soluble fiber is often recommended for gut comfort and smoother digestion.

Fiber and the Gut Microbiome
Fermentable fibers feed healthy gut bacteria, which produce short-chain fatty acids that protect colon cells and reduce inflammation. This document explains why fiber is essential for maintaining microbial diversity and gut health.

Effects of Low-Fiber Diets
Too little fiber can cause irregular bowel movements, digestive discomfort, and reduced microbial diversity. Gradually increasing fiber intake supports digestive comfort and balance.
