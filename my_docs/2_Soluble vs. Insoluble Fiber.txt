Overview: Soluble Fiber and Insoluble Fiber
This document explains the difference between soluble fiber and insoluble fiber, two major categories of dietary fiber. Soluble fiber dissolves in water and forms a gel-like texture during digestion, while insoluble fiber does not dissolve and adds bulk to stool.

Functions of Soluble Fiber
Soluble fiber slows digestion, helps steady blood sugar, and lowers LDL (“bad”) cholesterol. Foods high in soluble fiber include oats, apples, berries, citrus fruits, beans, and lentils. These benefits explain why many people look for “foods rich in soluble fiber” to support heart and metabolic health.

Functions of Insoluble Fiber
Insoluble fiber speeds the movement of food through the digestive tract, prevents constipation, and supports regular bowel habits. Foods rich in insoluble fiber include whole grains, nuts, seeds, and vegetables.

Why Both Types of Fiber Are Important
Most plant foods contain a mix of both fiber types. Soluble fiber supports cholesterol and blood sugar control, while insoluble fiber promotes bowel regularity—making both essential for overall health.
