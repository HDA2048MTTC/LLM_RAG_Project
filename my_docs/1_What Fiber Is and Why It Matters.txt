Definition of Dietary Fiber
This document explains the definition of dietary fiber and why the body cannot digest dietary fiber. Dietary fiber is the indigestible part of plant foods that the body lacks enzymes to break down. For this reason, fiber moves through the digestive system mostly unchanged, while still playing an important role in digestion, blood sugar control, and heart health.

Why Fiber Matters for Daily Health
Fiber-rich foods—such as fruits, vegetables, whole grains, nuts, seeds, and beans—help you feel full longer and support healthy eating habits. Many people search for “high-fiber foods” or “fiber benefits,” and this document highlights why fiber is important for overall comfort and long-term wellness.

General Health Benefits of Dietary Fiber
Dietary fiber supports regular bowel movements, steadier energy, and lower cholesterol levels. Research links higher fiber intake with lower risks of heart disease, diabetes, and digestive problems. Understanding what dietary fiber is helps people improve daily eating habits and overall health.
